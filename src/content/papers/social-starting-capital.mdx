## Overview

People do not choose the families, assets, legal systems, public institutions, or historical circumstances into which they are born. Yet those conditions shape their capabilities long before individual effort can plausibly explain the resulting differences.

This paper argues that part of society's productive inheritance—the value attributable to nature, accumulated knowledge, public institutions, social cooperation, and historically created scarcity rents—may legitimately finance a material starting stake for every child.

The **Social Starting Capital Framework (SSCF)** separates three functions that are often conflated:

- A universal child-development floor delivered through services or guaranteed provision
- Supplementary household support that recognizes both fixed costs and economies of scale
- An independently owned birthright capital account for every child

## A Budget-Closed Model

The revised framework gives every eligible newborn an equal, strictly positive statutory base grant. Sibling count can affect supplementary household support, but it never reduces the child's independent capital right.

All variable bonuses and progressive top-ups are normalized to explicit pools. Realized births, household composition, and income reports therefore cannot cause authorized expenditure to be exceeded. A transparent general-revenue backstop makes the statutory minimum a genuine guarantee instead of a residual aspiration.

The model also distinguishes maximum spending capacity from actual expenditure, specifies how spending is divided between current revenue and fund withdrawals, records withdrawals in child-account dynamics, and prevents public fund assets from being counted twice when they correspond to child-account liabilities.

## Intergenerational Sustainability

The fund is evaluated in real per-capita terms rather than by nominal non-decline. The paper derives an exact sustainability condition incorporating investment returns, saved inflows, actual withdrawals, inflation, and population growth.

A full calibration and demographic stress test demonstrate that:

1. The protected base survives a birth shock.
2. Only the variable cohort bonus adjusts when births exceed expectations.
3. Actual fund withdrawals remain below the statutory ceiling.
4. Every unit of spending is reconciled through a complete accounting identity.

The result is a pre-distributional institution: each generation inherits a protected material foothold in a social world it did not create but must be able to enter as an agent.
